Abun

A language of the turtle coast of Papua, Indonesia

Its speakers call it Abun or Anden.

// who and where

On the Bird's Head coast

  • Abun is spoken along the northern coast of the Bird's Head Peninsula, in Tambrauw Regency, Southwest Papua, Indonesia.
  • About 3,000 speakers (counted in 1995), in around 20 villages and hamlets.
  • It has several dialects: Abun Tat, Abun Ye and Abun Ji.
  • Neighbours have other names for it, like Madik or Yimbun. One outsiders' name, "Karon Pantai," is considered insulting, so it's best to use the speakers' own names: Abun or Anden.
TAMBRAUW, SW PAPUA

// family tree

A language on its own

  • Abun is not closely related to any other language. Most experts now call it a language isolate; some link it to a "West Papuan" family.
  • Its neighbours include Moi, Mpur and Maybrat, all very different languages.
  • Living by the sea, Abun speakers borrowed sea and boat words from Biak, an Austronesian language of seafarers.

// the turtle coast

Home of the giants

  • Two Abun-area beaches, Jamursba Medi and Wermon (locally Jeen Yessa and Jeen Syuab), host the largest remaining nesting site of Pacific leatherback turtles, the world's biggest turtles.
  • About 75% of all leatherback nesting in the western Pacific happens on this coast.
  • Local communities guard nests and run Turtle Camps for children.
