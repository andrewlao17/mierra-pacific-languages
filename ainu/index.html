Ainu

The Indigenous language of Hokkaido, Japan

Today's first word: irankarapte, a greeting

Who and where

Who speaks Ainu?

  • The Ainu are the Indigenous people of Hokkaido. Ainu was also once spoken on Sakhalin, the Kuril Islands and in northern Honshu.
  • The word aynu means "human".
  • A language isolate: it has no proven relatives, and it is not related to Japanese.
  • Today it is endangered, with very few native speakers left.

Oral literature

Stories that are sung

Ainu was traditionally passed on by speaking and singing, not writing.

yukarlong epics of human heroes
kamuy yukarsongs of the gods
uwepekerprose folktales
  • In a kamuy yukar, a god or spirit (often an animal) tells its own story in the first person. Women usually sang them.
  • The owl is kotan kor kamuy, the god who guards the village.

The owl god's song

In 1922, 19-year-old Chiri Yukie wrote down 13 songs of the gods in Ainu, with Japanese translations. She died hours after finishing; her book appeared in 1923.

History

Why is Ainu endangered?

For more than a century, the Ainu faced forced assimilation and discrimination that nearly erased their culture.

1899

A law pushes Ainu people to assimilate; Japanese replaces Ainu in daily life.

1994

Kayano Shigeru becomes the first Ainu member of parliament, and asks questions in Ainu.

1997

The 1899 law is repealed; a new law supports Ainu language and culture.

2008

Parliament adopts a resolution recognizing the Ainu as an Indigenous people.

2019

A national law recognizes the Ainu as Indigenous for the first time.

Ainu today

Bringing it back

2020
Upopoy, the National Ainu Museum and Park, opens. Its name means "singing together in a large group."
2013
The Irankarapte campaign starts encouraging people across Hokkaido to use the Ainu greeting.
On air
A radio course teaches Ainu, with free lesson books, alongside community classes.
