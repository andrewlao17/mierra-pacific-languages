A family of languages from New Guinea's swampy southwest coast, home of world-famous woodcarvers

Who and where

A family of a dozen languages

  • The Asmat–Kamoro languages are spoken in the coastal swamps and mangrove forests of southwest New Guinea, in Indonesia.
  • The family includes the Asmat languages (Central, Casuarina Coast, North, Citak and others), Kamoro, Sempan and Buruwai.
  • They belong to Trans–New Guinea, one of the largest language families in the world.

An origin story

Wood is life

For the Asmat, people and trees are closely linked, and wood is the source of life. In one origin story, the first being, Fumeripits, built the first men's house, the jeu. Lonely, he carved human figures from trees. But they didn't move…

So he made a drum from a hollowed tree and lizard skin. As he played, the figures' elbows came unstuck from their knees, and they began to dance.

Think about it

Whose story is it?

  • Bisj poles were made to honour people who had died, sometimes in conflicts between villages, and to help restore balance to the community.
  • Parts of the carving process involve secret ceremonies, so we only describe them here.
  • Traditionally, poles were left to decay near sago palms, so that their power would return to the earth and help the sago grow.
  • Today, many poles stand in museums around the world. Some were collected in 1961 and are now in New York.

Hope

Carving a future

1973
The Asmat Museum of Culture and Progress opens in Agats.
Every year
An Asmat cultural festival, usually in October, with a woodcarving competition, weaving, music, dance and canoe races.
Today
A carving association links carvers with sustainable forest use, because carving needs healthy forests.
  • The forests that carvers depend on face threats like deforestation. Protecting forests helps protect the culture, and the words that go with it.
  • Recording the smaller languages, like Sempan, before more knowledge is lost is still urgent work.
