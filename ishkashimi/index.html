Ishkashimi

škošmī zəvuk

One small language on two sides of a river, and of an international border

Follow the boat downstream.

Who and where

At the bend in the river

  • Ishkashimi is spoken near the place where the Panj River turns sharply north.
  • Afghanistan: villages around Ishkashim town.
  • Tajikistan: mainly Ryn, plus some families in nearby villages.
  • Its speakers call it škošmī zəvuk or rənīzəvuk.
  • UNESCO's atlas describes it as severely endangered.
about 1,500 in Afghanistanabout 1,000 in Tajikistan

A sun with an ancient name

The Ishkashimi word for sun is rémuzd. Scholars trace it back to Ahura Mazda, the supreme god in Zoroastrianism, an ancient religion of Iran and Central Asia.

Over many centuries, the name of a god became an everyday word. Today, most Ishkashimi speakers are Ismaili Muslims.

Pronunciation tips
  • ə is a short vowel like the a in "about."
  • γ is a soft sound made far back in the mouth.
  • c sounds like ts in "cats."

Pronunciation guides are approximate. These words come from written descriptions, not recordings.

Grammar puzzle

The ending that can travel

In many languages, an ending stays on the verb. In past-tense Ishkashimi, the ending for "I" can move to another word in the sentence!

Detective tip: Follow the red -m. It marks who did the action, even when it's not on the verb.

Do attitudes change what we hear?

Why it matters now

Can families keep the language going?

In the 2007 Afghan survey, Dari was the main language for school, religion, media and government. Many children spoke Dari with each other. Only about half of the people asked expected their children to use Ishkashimi as adults.

Parents wanted books
Almost everyone surveyed said Ishkashimi books would help, and many wanted literacy classes.
