Where and who

An island at the edge of the map

  • Yonaguni is Japan's westernmost inhabited island. Islanders call it Dunan, and their language Dunan munui, "Dunan words."
  • It is a Ryukyuan language. It is related to Japanese, but Japanese speakers can't understand it. Japan's government still calls it a "dialect."
  • About 1,700 people live on the island. In 2015, about 400 spoke the language, almost all of them older adults.
  • UNESCO lists it as severely endangered.

Language lab

The sound-change machine

Japanese and Dunan munui grew from the same ancestor language. Over centuries, Dunan sounds changed in regular ways. Dunan munui has only three main vowels, a, i, u, instead of Japanese's five. Pick a Japanese word, then click the rules to turn it into Dunan munui.

Even the island's two names, Japanese Yonaguni and local Dunan, are related. Real languages have exceptions, so these rules don't work for every word.

Counting

One, two, three… what?

To count things in Dunan munui, you add a counter word that depends on what you're counting. The ending -ci is for everyday objects:

3

Writing without an alphabet

Kaidā: pictures that kept count

  • In the 1600s, rulers placed a heavy head tax on the islands. Every adult owed goods like grain and cloth.
  • Few islanders could read or write Japanese or Chinese characters. So they used kaidā: about 70–80 picture signs for goods, plus marks for numbers and family symbols.
  • Anyone could "read" a kaidā record, whatever language they spoke. It was used until Japanese schooling arrived in the late 1800s.

Nae Ikema, born in 1919, was one of the last people with detailed knowledge of these signs.

Island life

Small horses and giant moths

The Yonaguni horse is one of only eight horse breeds native to Japan. These small, gentle horses once worked the fields. When tractors arrived, their numbers crashed. In 1976, the town named them a natural treasure.

The mystery under the sea

In 1986, a diver off Yonaguni found giant underwater rocks with flat "terraces," straight edges and steps. People have argued ever since: natural, or carved by people long ago? Vote first, then flip the evidence cards.

Why it matters now

Will children speak Dunan munui?

  • School, TV and phones are in Japanese. Most children grow up speaking only Japanese.
  • Yonaguni has no high school, so most teens leave the island at age 15, often for good.
  • Calling it a "dialect" made many people think it wasn't worth protecting.
