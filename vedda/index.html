Vedda

The language of Sri Lanka's forest people, where a ship can be "the vehicle of the ocean"

Today, you'll speak in riddles.

Who and where

The people of the forest

  • Vedda comes from a Tamil word linked to hunting. Many Vedda people call themselves Wanniyalaeto, "people of the forest."
  • The language is mainly linked with Dambana, in Sri Lanka's Uva Province, near Maduru Oya National Park.
  • Traditional life included hunting, gathering yams and collecting wild honey from trees and rocks.
  • Younger generations increasingly use Sinhala or Tamil first. Experts rate the language's danger differently, but all agree it is endangered.
about 300 speakers (older, undated figure)about 2,500 Vedda people (2002)

One recent study says about 1,000 speakers. Exact counts are uncertain.

Mystery

Why did outsiders disagree?

Visitors gave opposite reports for nearly 200 years. Click each one in order:

A creole?A new language formed when older Vedda speech mixed with early Sinhala and Tamil.
A language isolate?Some linguists see no relationship to Dravidian or Indo-European.
Indo-Aryan or a dialect?Ethnologue groups it with Indo-Aryan, and others link it closely to Sinhala.

Linguists still debate this. The original Vedda language or languages have no known origin.

Speak along

Hear the cc and jj

Vedda is known for many cc and jj sounds, made with the middle of your tongue against the roof of your mouth.

  1. Say "chee." Now make the start shorter and crisper.
  2. Say "jee" the same way, without a buzz.
  3. Hold cc for a moment, then release: dee-ya-rac-ca.
  4. Hold jj, then release: ee-ra-poj-ja.

Forest under pressure

When the forest changed

1881

The census counts 2,200 Veddas.

1959

A linguist finds that the language is mostly limited to Dambana's older generation.

1977

The Mahaweli development scheme takes in large areas of traditional hunting land.

1983

Maduru Oya becomes a national park. Hunting there is banned, and people are moved out.

A leader who was moved to a rice-farming village said the government took their hunting tools and "gave us mammoties to dig our own graves."

A mammoty is a heavy digging hoe.

Today

Still speaking, still leading

  • May 2026: Dambana leader Uru Warige Wannila Aththo handed a bow and axe to Nishantha Kumara, the new Rathugala leader.
  • June 2026: He met Sri Lanka's prime minister about access to ancestral lands and needs at Dambana School.
  • 2020: A linguist recorded Vedda grammar with five native speakers in Dambana, including two young bilingual speakers.
  • Vedda words still appear in hunting talk and ceremonies in some communities.
  • Linguists also think pre-Sinhala languages left traces in Sinhala itself, possibly in words such as gala, "stone." This idea is debated.
