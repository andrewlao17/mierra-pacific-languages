Jejueo

The language of Korea's volcanic island

Today's first phrase: honjeo opseoye, "welcome!"

Where and who

An island language

  • Spoken on Jeju, a volcanic island off the southern tip of Korea.
  • A Koreanic language: related to Korean, but most Korean speakers can't understand it.
  • UNESCO and Jeju's own laws call it a separate language. South Korea's national language institute still officially calls it a dialect.
  • UNESCO lists it as critically endangered (2010), with about 5,000–10,000 fluent speakers, most of them elderly.
Jeju Island, South Korea

A letter from 1446

The dot that survived

Korean is written in Hangul, published under King Sejong in 1446. One of its original vowels was a single dot, ㆍ, called arae-a ("lower a").

Standard Korean lost the sound and later stopped writing it. Jejueo still has the sound, and Jeju's official spelling rules (2014) still use the letter.

Haenyeo, the women divers of Jeju

Women of the sea

The haenyeo

  • Haenyeo ("sea women") dive about 10 m down with no oxygen tanks, gathering shellfish like abalone and sea urchins. Some are in their 80s.
  • They hold their breath for about a minute or two per dive. Surfacing, they let out a whistling breath called sumbisori.
  • Divers are ranked by skill, not age, and the most skilled lead the others:
hagunbeginners
junggunmiddle rank
sanggunthe most skilled

In 2016, UNESCO added haenyeo culture to its list of the world's Intangible Cultural Heritage.

Under pressure

Why is Jejueo fading?

  • The violence of the 1948 Jeju uprising, the Korean War and fast modernization badly disrupted island life and its language.
  • For a long time, Jejueo was looked down on as inferior to Standard Korean.
  • Many fluent speakers are now over 70, and few children grow up speaking it.
  • Haenyeo culture, which carries many Jejueo words and songs, is shrinking too.

Hope

Keeping Jejueo alive

2007
Jeju passes a law to preserve and promote the Jeju language.
2014
Official Jejueo spelling rules, keeping the arae-a.
2017–19
Jejueo textbooks and the first English-language book on the language.
  • Children's storybooks, newspaper stories and a bimonthly magazine in Jejueo.
  • A talking dictionary with recordings of speakers.
  • New haenyeo, from their 20s to their 50s, train at the Hansupul Haenyeo School, open since 2008.
