Who and where

Ten tribes, ten languages

  • The Andaman Islands lie in the Bay of Bengal. They belong to India, but sit closer to Myanmar and Indonesia.
  • There were once ten tribes, each with its own language. Neighbours understood each other, but people at opposite ends of the islands could not.
  • Present Great Andamanese blends four northern languages: Jeru, Bo, Khora and Sare. Its grammar comes mostly from Jeru.
  • It is counted as India's sixth language family. Geneticists link Andaman Islanders to one of the earliest migrations out of Africa, about 60,000 years ago.

How endangered?

From thousands to a few dozen

About 5,000 people (estimates range from about 2,000 to 8,000)

Each dot stands for about 10 people. Click a year.

  • Britain set up a penal colony at Port Blair in 1858. The islanders fought back, including at the Battle of Aberdeen in 1859.
  • Hundreds died in the fighting. Thousands more died from measles, flu and other diseases new to the islands.
  • Around 1970, the survivors were moved to tiny Strait Island. By 2020, about three elders still spoke Jeru. The children speak Hindi.

The big idea

A grammar built on the body

Linguist Anvita Abbi found that Great Andamanese divides the body into seven zones. Small markers for those zones attach to nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs.

er=bungoi beautiful on the outside

e=bungoi beautiful on the inside

Words from nature, like ra (pig), usually stand alone. Abbi argues that no other known language builds its grammar this way.

Knowledge that saves lives

The sea has turned upside down

On 26 December 2004, a huge tsunami struck the Indian Ocean. Press the button to follow what happened on Strait Island.

  1. Nao Jr., a Great Andamanese elder, saw fish from the deep sea come up to the surface.
  2. He understood the danger and shouted a warning in his own language.
  3. The elders ran to a small hill and waited there for days until rescuers arrived.
  4. Nao Jr. helped six children climb a tree. He swam around it for seven hours, telling them when to climb higher and when to come down.

The warning came from knowing the sea. When a language dies, that kind of knowledge can die with it.

Stories and wildlife

Birds are ancestors

The Great Andamanese do not hunt or eat birds, because they see them as ancestors. Boa Sr., the last speaker of Bo, spoke to birds in her language when no person could answer her.

Racing the clock

Saving the last voices

2008

A Book of Letters for Andamanese children and the first CD of the community's songs are released.

2009

Nao Jr., keeper of stories and bird names, dies. So does Boro, last speaker of Khora.

2010

Boa Sr., last speaker of Bo, dies at about 85.

2011

Anvita Abbi publishes a dictionary with sound files, followed by a full grammar in 2013.

2020

Licho, last speaker of Sare, dies. About three speakers of Jeru remain.

  • The speakers made this record possible. When Abbi finally cracked the grammar and tested it by phone, Licho told her: "you have caught our language!"
  • Recordings are now archived, and basic lessons reached young children at the Strait Island nursery school.
